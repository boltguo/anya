# IELTS Speaking Band Descriptors (Public Version)

Four criteria, each scored 0–9. Final band = average, rounded to nearest 0.5.

In text-based practice, score only the first three — mark Pronunciation as **N/A** and average over three.

---

## 1. Fluency and Coherence (25%)

Can the learner speak at length without noticeable effort, and do ideas connect logically?

| Band | Descriptor (condensed) |
|---|---|
| 9 | Speaks fluently with only rare repetition or self-correction. Hesitation related to content, not language. Coherent, appropriately extended. |
| 8 | Fluent with only occasional repetition/self-correction. Hesitation usually content-related. Develops topics coherently and appropriately. |
| 7 | Speaks at length without noticeable effort or loss of coherence. Some language-related hesitation. Uses a range of connectives and discourse markers with some flexibility. |
| 6 | Willing to speak at length, though may lose coherence at times due to occasional repetition, self-correction, or hesitation. Uses a range of connectives but not always appropriately. |
| 5 | Usually maintains flow but uses repetition, self-correction, and/or slow speech to keep going. Overuses certain connectives and discourse markers. Produces simple speech fluently but more complex communication causes problems. |
| 4 | Cannot respond without noticeable pauses. May speak slowly with frequent repetition and self-correction. Links basic sentences but with repetitious use of simple connectives and some breakdown in coherence. |

**Text-based signals:**
- **Band 7+**: extended responses (3-5+ sentences on Part 1 questions, sustained Part 2 monologue with all bullet points covered, multi-sentence Part 3 answers with clear reasoning).
- **Band 6**: adequate responses but some one-sentence answers, occasional awkward transitions.
- **Band 5**: many short responses, visible struggle to extend, repeated sentence starters.

---

## 2. Lexical Resource (25%)

Vocabulary range, flexibility, and precision in spoken English.

| Band | Descriptor (condensed) |
|---|---|
| 9 | Uses vocab with full flexibility and precision in all topics. Idiomatic language used naturally and accurately. |
| 8 | Wide range to convey precise meaning. Uses less common/idiomatic vocab skillfully with occasional inaccuracies. Effective paraphrase as required. |
| 7 | Uses range flexibly to discuss a variety of topics. Uses some less common/idiomatic vocab with awareness of style and collocation; occasional inappropriate choices. Paraphrases effectively. |
| 6 | Has a wide enough range to discuss topics at length and make meaning clear, despite inappropriacies. Generally paraphrases successfully. |
| 5 | Manages to talk about familiar and unfamiliar topics but with limited flexibility. Attempts paraphrase with mixed success. |
| 4 | Able to talk about familiar topics but only conveys basic meaning on unfamiliar topics. Frequent inappropriate word choice. Rarely attempts paraphrase. |

**What to listen for:**
- Topic-specific vocab → band 7+ ("in remote areas", "carbon footprint", "peer pressure").
- Paraphrase when stuck → "you know, that thing where…" → band 5-6 if frequent.
- Idiomatic expressions used correctly → band 7-8. Used incorrectly or mechanically → stays at 6.

---

## 3. Grammatical Range and Accuracy (25%)

| Band | Descriptor (condensed) |
|---|---|
| 9 | Uses full range of structures naturally and appropriately. Produces consistently accurate structures apart from minor slips. |
| 8 | Uses wide range flexibly. Majority of sentences error-free, with only very occasional inappropriacies or basic/non-systematic errors. |
| 7 | Uses a range of complex structures with some flexibility. Frequently produces error-free sentences, though some grammatical mistakes persist. |
| 6 | Uses a mix of simple and complex structures, but with limited flexibility. May make frequent mistakes with complex structures, though these rarely cause comprehension problems. |
| 5 | Produces basic sentence forms with reasonable accuracy. Uses a limited range of more complex structures, but these usually contain errors and may cause some comprehension problems. |
| 4 | Produces basic sentence forms and some correct simple sentences but subordinate structures are rare. Errors are frequent and may lead to misunderstanding. |

**Key distinctions vs. Writing:**
- Spoken grammar is looser. Fragments, false starts, and contractions are **normal** and **not** penalized.
- Look for **range** (conditionals, perfect tenses, passive, reported speech) before accuracy — a learner who only uses present simple can't score above 5 even if perfectly accurate.

---

## 4. Pronunciation (25%) — N/A for text-based practice

Mark as **N/A** and compute final band from the other three criteria.

If the learner wants pronunciation feedback, suggest they record themselves using a phone voice memo and self-evaluate against the band descriptors — the key features are individual sounds, word stress, sentence stress, intonation, and chunking. Text-based assessment cannot evaluate these; use `speaking-azure-score` for audio-based pronunciation grading.

For reference only (condensed):
| Band | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 9 | Full range of features used with precision. Sustained effective communication. |
| 8 | Wide range used with flexibility. Only occasional lapses. L1 accent present but does not affect intelligibility. |
| 7 | All positive band 6 features and some band 8 features. Generally easy to understand. |
| 6 | Range of pronunciation features with mixed control. Shows some effective use but with lapses. Generally understood, though individual words/sounds may reduce clarity. |
| 5 | Shows all positive band 4 and some band 6 features. |
| 4 | Limited range. Attempts to control features but lapses frequent. Mispronunciations cause some difficulty for the listener. |

---

## Scoring shortcut

As with Writing, apply the "best fit" rule: pick the band matching the majority of the criteria. Don't require every feature of a band to be present.

Also: real examiners grade **holistically across all three parts of Speaking**, not per-part. If you've run a full mock, give one overall band, not three.
